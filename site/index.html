The gap never closes

Across 65 months, Kerala’s wholesale price was never below the national one. The median month: tomato 1.74x, onion 1.85x, potato 2.53x.

What a buyer can do with this

  1. Watch Kolar, Pimpalgaon and Bangalore, not Kerala. The hub moves first in most spikes, by about two weeks.
  2. Buy forward when the model alerts. Hotels, cooperatives and Supplyco can contract or stock before the peak.
  3. Compare nearby markets every day. Half the time a tomato market within 40 km is 20% cheaper, and Malabar markets usually undercut the south.
  4. Do not lean on the news. Adding news coverage to the model made its warnings worse, not better.

How it was built

Every number here is rebuilt from the raw files by scripts in the repository.

Data

26,504,208 Agmarknet records (2021 to May 2026) from India Data Portal, ISB, under ODC-By. Agmarknet’s own site and the Consumer Affairs price portal sit behind a CAPTCHA and were not scraped. Rainfall and temperature at each hub from the Open-Meteo archive. News volume from the GDELT API, fetched with Scrapling at one request per five seconds.

Cleaning

3.23 million tomato, onion and potato records. The two sources and the old source’s two market-code systems were merged into 2,007 markets, and duplicate reports removed. Market-days more than three times off their state’s median were dropped: under 2% in Kerala, including one market reporting a flat Rs 270/kg for weeks. Thirteen data tests run on every build.

Limits

These are wholesale prices, not shop prices. Kerala’s premium is not a margin. The test period holds only five spikes, so the warning’s hit rate is a small sample. Correlation in prices is not proof of where a shock started.
